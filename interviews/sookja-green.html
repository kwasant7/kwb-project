Interview with Sookja Green: A Life of Courage, Love, and Two Cultures

Interviewed August 25, 2026 | Seattle, Washington | 46 minutes | In Korean with English translation

Speaking mostly in Korean, Sookja Green tells how she met her husband at the Embassy Club on Yongsan Garrison, what it took to leave Korea, the prejudice she faced and stood up to in America, and raising a son proud of both his cultures.

Background

Sookja Green was born Sookja Lee in Korea in 1955. Her father worked on a US Army base, and she grew up around the American soldiers who visited their home. After her family’s fortunes collapsed she left university and worked as a pianist, and in 1987, at the Embassy Club on Yongsan Garrison, she met Brent Green, an Army JAG officer. They married in May 1988 and she came to the United States that June. Through two decades of military moves, from Virginia to Georgia, Hawaii and Arizona, she met prejudice with courage and raised her son to treasure both of his cultures.
